Exit Gmail

The address every other account resets to — owned by someone else.

// why

  • Email is your master key: every other account resets through it, so whoever runs your inbox holds the root of your online identity.
  • Your address is the lock-in: leaving means re-pointing every service, contact, and 2FA that depends on it — by hand.
  • Terms of service rated “D” — see the documented points at ToS;DR

// first, get your stuff out

  1. Export everything via takeout.google.com — select Mail (as MBOX) and Contacts (as vCard).
  2. Import that archive into your new provider over IMAP, or keep the MBOX as an offline backup you fully control.

// exit paths

[switch] Switch to private email

Proton Mail

The default like-for-like — end-to-end encrypted, Swiss-based, full webmail/app/calendar. Easiest landing if you want everything in one place.

via privacy-guides awesome-privacy switching-software
Tuta open source

Encrypts more of the mailbox than most (subject lines, contacts) — the pick when maximal encryption matters, at the cost of no standard IMAP/PGP interop.

via privacy-guides awesome-privacy switching-software
Mailbox.org

A full Office-style suite (mail, calendar, files) on standard IMAP — best if you want a clean Google-Workspace replacement without lock-in.

via privacy-guides awesome-privacy switching-software

[self-host] Run your own mail server

Mailcow

A complete dockerized mail stack. Total ownership — but deliverability is a constant fight, and one misconfigured record sends you to everyone's spam folder. Only if you mean it.

via awesome-privacy
Mail-in-a-box

The gentlest self-host on-ramp — one script sets up the whole server. Still yours to maintain and keep off blocklists.

via awesome-privacy
